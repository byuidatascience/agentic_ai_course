# Learning Arcs

## The Story

Unit 1: This is your chance to build the powerful agent of your dreams. We are arming you right from the start with the coolest tools possible, and you can make your own personalized assistant.

Unit 2: You have been hired at ACME.io, the one stop shop for everything tech related. They hired you to diagnose and fix an agent they have already made. You're going to excel in your new role and report everything you find. Even if it scares you...

Unit 3: Armed with what you know about safety and architecture, it is time to plan your new startup's agents from the ground up with reliability and safety in mind. Blow the competition away with something that really works.

## Student-Facing Names

Unit 1: **Build**

Unit 2: **Diagnose**

Unit 3: **Design**

Students never hear that their Unit 1 agent is "bad" during Unit 1. That framing hurts morale. After Unit 1 the reveal comes gradually, across Units 2 and 3, not in one announcement: it wasn't as good as they thought, and now they can make it better.

Unit 3 language: use "architect" and "engineer" throughout, as in becoming a *true* agent engineer. The point is that they weren't fully engineering before. In Unit 3, they turn fuzzy, ambiguous business needs into architectural decisions with metrics and safety standards. Context engineering is the term they learn inside the unit, not the unit's name.

## The Path (instructor only)

Unit 1: Build a **"Bad"** Agent

    Increasing complexity, API, tools, MCP, plugins

Unit 2: **"Why"** was it bad?

    Does it work? (Does what was asked)

    Is it aligned? (Does what was implied, moral, safe/harmless)

    Is it ethical? (Has inherently moral cause)

Unit 3: Build a **"Good"** Agent

    Armed with knowledge, you set out to reclaim your good name

    The power is in you, here is some support as you exercise creation
